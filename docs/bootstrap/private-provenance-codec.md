# Private provenance wire and crypto reuse

`src/private-provenance-codec.ts` is an internal byte-format module shared by the existing synthetic fixture wrapper and future fixed production consumers. It is not exported from the package entry point. This extraction preserves envelope/receipt version 1, sorted canonical JSON, exact byte limits, full-message Ed25519 signatures, AES-256-GCM with a fresh 12-byte nonce and 16-byte tag, header AAD, and the existing base-derived KMS context. The committed fabricated vector was produced by the unchanged `f286dd054112128eeb1b2c3909141af10a354a2b` implementation.

The codec accepts explicit public envelope bytes, expected repository/commit/run, trusted public keys, receipt bytes, and ephemeral key material. `privateProvenanceBinding` verifies the public signature and exact expected subject/run. `verifyPrivateReceipt` verifies the receipt signature over the entire canonical payload and its exact public binding. `parsePrivateEnvelope` checks its whole-byte digest against the receipt, framing, public binding, KMS key ARN, and wrapped-key digest before a consumer requests unwrap. `decryptPrivateEnvelope` repeats those checks and releases an owned plaintext buffer only after GCM authentication succeeds; it clears its partial plaintext buffers on success or failure.

Encryption and decryption borrow caller key/input buffers without clearing or retaining them. The caller must clear transferred or locally copied data keys in `finally`, and owns and must clear returned plaintext. Canonical parsing clears its owned byte copy but cannot erase immutable JavaScript strings or parsed values. No codec return type is proof of storage, capture admission, signer enrollment, read approval, audit durability, identity, or authorization. Wire-valid timestamps and retention fields do not prove current provider policy or enforcement. Callers must establish those facts independently and sanitize errors at their outer boundary.

A fixed production capture consumer must admit only the approved explicit summary categories before encoding; acquire its authorized key and signing material, verify exact-version storage/retention after write, and only then sign its receipt. A fixed read consumer must authenticate configuration/identity, verify the public/receipt/frame bindings, enforce scoped single-use approval and durable audit before unwrap/plaintext release, verify provider storage/retention evidence, and validate decrypted summary schema. No provider callbacks, enrollment hooks, permissive success flags, network operations, deployment wiring, or real capture/read operation are introduced here.

The existing synthetic API keeps every `syntheticOnly: true` guard, `synthetic-fixture` category restriction, adapter sequencing, receipt time window, approval/audit checks, sanitized errors, and transferred-key ownership. Importing the codec does not authorize a production consumer to invoke or bypass those synthetic APIs. Production composition remains separate source work under ADR-0060 and its accepted design.
